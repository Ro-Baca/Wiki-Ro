## Guy Hoffman (Cornell University):
http://guyhoffman.com
[Google scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=ZocLOAMAAAAJ&view_op=list_works&sortby=pubdate)

Develeped non-antropomorphic robots such as **Shimon** and **Blossom**, changing the paradigm of rigid articulations for kinematics based in expresive animations.

His publications are related to mechanical design and stetic design, working with social robots with unconventional morphologies, flexible mechanisms, tensor structures and abstract morphologies. Introduced the **compliant mechanisms**.

Most cited:
- _"Cost-based anticipatory action selection for human-robot fluency"_ (2007).
- _"Blossom: A Tensile Social Robot Design with a Handcrafted Shell"_ (2018).

## Takayuki Kanda (Kyoto University):
https://www.robot.soc.i.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kanda/
[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=BL9EACgAAAAJ&view_op=list_works&sortby=pubdate)

Codesign **Robovie** robots at ART, and studied how the hight, base movement and sensor arrays.
Wrote **"Human-Robot Interaction in Social Robotics"** 

Leads the HRI lab at Kyoto University, his research focuses on kinematics of robots in public spaces, physical spaces, proportions and mechanisms affects human behaviour.
His research defined mechanics for microphone arrays configurations and positioning in the chasis for optimal navigation.

Most cited:
- _"A communication robot in a shopping mall: Empirical estimation of behaviors and design effects"_ (2010). 
- _"Human-Robot Interaction in Social Robotics"_.

## Hiroshi Ishiguro (Osaka University):
https://eng.irl.sys.es.osaka-u.ac.jp
[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=BNhvcyYAAAAJ&hl=en)

Developed _Geminoid_, _Erica_ and _Telenoid_ robots, defined the concept of **extreme morphology (hiperrealism vs minimum morphology)** 

His Lab studies hiperrealist hardware design with **Geminoides** as well as minimum morphology with platforms such as **Telenoid** and **Hugvie**. Focusing in how design of textures and physical structure affects _precence_.

Most cited:
- _The uncanny advantage of using androids in cognitive and social science research"_ (2006).
- _"Interactive robots as social partners and peer tutors for children"_ (2004).

## Karl MacDorman (Indiana University):
http://www.macdorman.com
[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=W-DuNjYAAAAJ&hl=en)

His research focuses in the **Uncanny Valley** effect, measuring how milimetrical changes in texture, facila simetry and actuator kinematics may provoke rejection or emphaty.

He defined quantifiable industrial design variables for measuring the Uncanny Valley effect and demostrated mathematically how mechanical discrepancies ( an eye actuator that moves slower than the neck, or hyper-realistic skin combined with a noisy motor) destroy interaction.

Most cited:
- _"Subjective ratings of robot video clips for human likeness, familiarity, and eeriness: An exploration of the uncanny valley"_ (2006). 
- _"Too real for comfort? Uncanny responses to computer generated faces"_ (2009).

## Wendy Ju (Cornell Tech):
https://www.wendyju.com
[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=kjYhZYwAAAAJ&hl=en)

# Future Research Direction

1. **More Realistic Research Scenarios**: Current research is still dominated by laboratory studies, lacking research in real and complex natural interaction scenarios, and the results obtained in laboratory environments may have limitations in terms of real-world applications. Research conducted in close-to-real-world contexts can help obtain more meaningful results and provide deeper insights into human behavior and robot interactions.
2. **Effective Measurement of Research Indicators**: Current research presents difficulties and challenges in obtaining reliable data to evaluate human–social robot interactions.Therefore, there are still some challenges in how to effectively and objectively measure the true level of such latent variables and improve the reliability, validity, and replicability of the data. This may require larger sample sizes, a more diverse set of approaches for measuring variables, and richer data types.
3. **Longitudinal Trial**: Current research is mainly based on short-term experiments, while interactions between humans and social robots often occur over the long term. Shortterm experiments cannot capture the long-term effects and how human–robot interaction changes over time. Future research could conduct more long-term longitudinal trials to provide more comprehensive data to support the conclusions, thus providing insights into the phenomenon and its mechanisms changing over time.
4. **More Specific Design Strategies**: Although the interaction design of social robots has accumulated a significant number of research findings, the current research is relatively scattered and has not yet formed a comprehensive design framework. *Basic research related to the robot interaction design and the user’s emotional experience is relatively lacking*, and the mapping mechanism between the interaction design factors and the healing effect is unclear. Therefore, in the future, it is necessary to study further the specific design features and specific design strategies of social robot interaction design, especially how the combination effect of different interaction design features will affect the efficiency of human–computer interaction and emotional experience.
5. **Uncovering the Psychological Cognitive Mechanisms behind User Behavior**: There is a close interplay between a user’s psychological cognitive process, their experience, and their behavior. Understanding the psychological cognitive process of users can help design social robots that better meet user needs and elicit positive emotions. The current empirical research usually only gives general conclusions.
